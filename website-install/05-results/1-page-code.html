Case study · Space Gyms

34 visits booked in 30 days. Half of them after the desk had closed.

Space Gyms runs two sites, in Dublin and Kildare. In October 2026 their assistant, Aria, started answering every enquiry. These are the first 30 days.

Summary

What happened at Space Gyms?

In its first 30 days, Aria booked 34 visits from 71 new contacts. 50% of those bookings were made outside staffed hours. In the first week alone she booked 15, all from the gym's website, before any social channels were switched on. No ads were run, and no staff time went into answering or booking.

Before

Good gym, normal problem.

Like most gyms, Space Gyms got enquiries through its website and social channels at all hours, and answered them when someone was free. Evening and weekend messages waited until the next shift. Coaches met prospects with little more than a first name.

After

Every enquiry answered, every consult prepared.

Aria now replies within seconds, introduces herself as the gym's digital assistant, asks a few quick questions and books a tour. She doesn't quote prices; she mentions there's an offer on and books the visit, so the conversation about membership happens in person.

The answers are attached to each booking, and coaches review them before the person walks in. Managers are notified the moment a visit is booked.

What it means for your gym

Why the after-hours number matters.

Half of the visits Aria booked would have waited until morning for a reply. Research on lead response shows a reply inside five minutes makes a lead 21 times more likely to qualify than one after thirty (MIT Lead Response Management Study). Those 17 after-hours bookings were people who, without an instant reply, would have been waiting until morning while other gyms answered.
